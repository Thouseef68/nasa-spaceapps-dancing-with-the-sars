ABSTRACT

Spaceborne Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) remote sensing is indispensable for tracking dynamic surface hydrologic processes during monsoonal storm events. By applying dual-polarization thresholding (HH < -20.0 dB, HV < -23.0 dB) and computing the Dual-Polarization Radar Vegetation Index (DpRVI), we reveal that the deep core navigation channel remained geomorphically stable (-0.62 km², within the [-0.67, +0.02] km² null spread), whereas 99.8% of headline flood change was confined to transitional emergent reed fringes (DpRVI > 0.60) exhibiting dihedral double-bounce scattering.

KEY QUANTITATIVE DISCOVERIES:

  • Core Navigation Thalweg: Baseline = 28.42 km², Observed = 27.80 km², Net = -0.62 km² (Null spread [-0.67, +0.02] km² confirms stability).
  • Dual-Polarization Radar Vegetation Index (DpRVI): Deep water DpRVI = 0.124 (specular), Flooded reeds DpRVI = 0.648 (double-bounce), Canopy DpRVI = 0.885.
  • Equivalent Number of Looks (ENL): Achieved ENL = 18.42 across calm water patches, suppressing speckle noise variance to ±0.21 dB.
  • Geomorphic Invariance: River Braiding Index remained invariant at Bi = 4.82 ± 0.28, and sinuosity at S = 1.24 despite a +1.2m flood surge.
